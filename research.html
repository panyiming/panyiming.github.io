Lendable Inventory Concentration, Recall Risk, and Equity Pricing Anomalies

SSRN

A short seller needs borrowed shares until the mispricing corrects, but lenders can recall them at any time. Where lendable inventory is concentrated (IC) across lenders, one lender's withdrawal can recall more shares than borrowers can replace in time. Within firms, a one-standard-deviation higher IC predicts a \chk{2.1} percentage point higher next-month recall probability (mean \chk{10.9}\%), controlling for the fee and lendable supply. This recall risk deters short selling: the same overpricing signal implied by anomalies attracts about one quarter as much short interest at the highest IC as at the lowest IC, although unused shares are available. As a result, less of the overpricing is corrected: monthly anomaly spreads are \chk{0.43}\% larger in the top than in the bottom IC quintile, mainly through the short leg, and remain \chk{0.33}\% larger net of fees. Russell 3000 inclusion, which broadens the lender base, lowers recall risk and raises short interest most in overpriced stocks with high pre-inclusion IC. Beyond the fee, inventory concentration makes access to lendable shares less durable, which deters short selling and sustains anomaly-related overpricing.
